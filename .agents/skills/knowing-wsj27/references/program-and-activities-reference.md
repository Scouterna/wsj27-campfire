# WSJ27 Program and Activities Reference

This reference describes the WSJ27 educational program and on-site experience, drawn mainly from the official Bulletins (Bulletin 1, August 2024; Bulletin 3, November 2025; Bulletin 4, July 2026) and jamboree2027.org. It covers the "Bravely" theme and objectives, the symbolic framework, the four program components and their modules, subcamp life, the Plaza, the IST experience, food, and health and safety. For event logistics see `jamboree2027-official-reference.md`; for how contingents are organized on site see `contingent-structure-reference.md`.

## Theme and Educational Objectives

The theme "Bravely" rests on three pillars, each with a mission and objectives (Bulletin 3 pp.32–34):

- You – self-discovery and growth: self-awareness, resilience, self-development (creativity, problem-solving, critical thinking, Scoutcraft), mental health and well-being, and balance between nature, technology, and online/offline life.
- You and Other People – diversity and collaboration: embracing diversity in all dimensions, communicating and collaborating across generations, cultures, and religions for peace, and building togetherness in Scouting.
- You and the World – active global citizenship: "positive activism", understanding environmental impact and conservation, and commitments toward the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

The program is framed by three action phrases: "GO FORWARD", "OVERCOME OBSTACLES", "BE BRAVE!".

## Symbolic Framework

The symbolic framework is built on storytelling (Bulletin 3 pp.44–46). Participants journey through the Modular Programme, whose modules are named after places (Beach, Village, Forest, City, River, Meadow). Subcamps are named after Polish flora, and hubs after four of those colors – Yellow, Red, Green, and Purple (the Bulletins' Symbolic Framework paragraph says "five colours"; it is wrong, see `sources-reference.md`). The story of "living bravely" is carried by "Companions" who travel with participants – each a character with a short story modeling a brave choice (for example Anna, Pola, Balbina, Cezary, Bogna, Mikolaj, Jan, Zofia, Seba, Aleks). The common thread: whatever each Companion does on the journey, they do it bravely.

## Program Components

The program has four components (Bulletin 3 pp.53–55):

- Subcamp Programme – daily life in the subcamps, including optional evening free-time activities, the Opening Game (first day), Cultural Day, and the Solidarity Badge (challenges exploring the legacy of "Solidarność").
- Modular Programme – the core themed activities, on-site and off-site, running 31 July – 8 August 2027; a structured rotation ensures every subcamp completes every module.
- Plaza Programme – walk-in cultural activities in the central Plaza / World Scout Centre (no registration; full patrols not required).
- Supporting Events – the Youth Forum (a Model UN-style debate), the 1 August "120 Years of Scouting" celebration and inter-religious ceremony, and the "Bravely" board game.
- Pre-Jamboree Task (new in Bulletin 4) – a pre-event global positive-action challenge tied to the three pillars (You; You and Other People; You and the World), aiming for 120,000 actions worldwide. It is open to units, patrols, or individuals and to all Scouts, and actions are reported on the Jamboree website with a live progress bar; the submission form and details are due in Bulletin 5 (around February 2027).

Program structure: participants usually take part as patrols; activities are pre-assigned to patrols; unit leaders receive daily briefings. Most modules run a morning (~3 hrs) and afternoon (~3 hrs) session; Meadow and Village are morning-only; Camp-in-Camp spans four sessions over two consecutive days; the last program day (8 August) has morning sessions only.

## Modular Programme Modules

On-site modules (Bulletin 3 pp.57, 63–70):

- Beach – physical and mental well-being (team sports, mindfulness; beach volleyball, kiting, resilience and time/resource workshops).
- Forest – bushcraft, woodcraft, pioneering, and campcraft (fire-making, knots, shelters, carving, axe work, tensegrity and camp-table pioneering).
- Kaleidoscope – a self-planned day for personal balance (creative workshops, role-play, mindfulness; e.g. Nature's Orchestra, culture bingo, genealogy, reflective art).
- Meadow – cultural diversity and spiritual development (critical-thinking games, handicrafts, teamwork art inspired by world cultures; morning-only).
- Village / Global Development Village – local and global citizenship (sustainability, social justice, SDGs; morning-only).

Off-site modules (Bulletin 3 p.58):

- Camp-in-Camp – a 24-hour community-service and outdoor adventure at Polish Scout camps (two consecutive days).
- City – Gdansk's culture and history through landmarks, museums, and guided challenges.
- River – aquatic adventure (kayaking, sailing) with maritime history and environmental conservation.

## Subcamp Life and the Plaza

Subcamps (Bulletin 3 pp.105–108): the site is organized into four hubs (Yellow, Red, Green, Purple), each with four subcamps (16 total). Each subcamp holds ~2,250 participants in ~56 units; a unit is four patrols camping together (up to 40 people, including leaders and carers), on an area of about 20 m x 25 m (500 m2). Units receive a dining shelter, tables and benches for 40, and cooking equipment. Participants bring their own tents, climate-appropriate sleeping bags (nights near 5 degrees C), mats, eating kit, water bottle, and Scout uniform. Daily Unit Leaders' Meetings cover information sharing, coordination, feedback, and community building.

Subcamp Programme (optional, evenings): free-time activities (sports, board games, social events), the Solidarity Badge, the first-day Opening Game, and Cultural Day (units present their heritage).

The Plaza (Bulletin 3 pp.60, 71–72) is the central marketplace, open to participants, IST, and day visitors. It hosts the World Scout Centre with Contingent Pavilions (National Tents), the One World Tent, the Faith and Beliefs Zone, Partners Pavilions (e.g. UN, WWF, ICRC), the Equality Zone, a Scouting Museum, the Jamboree Gate and all NSO flags, a Scout Shop, Food Houses, a Swap Centre, and a Silent Disco. All Plaza activities are walk-in. Beyond the on-site shop, official Jamboree merchandise (pins, badges, buffs, and the Jamboree Collection) is sold online at shop.jamboree2027.org – distinct from any national contingent's own profile package.

## The IST Experience

The International Service Team (IST) are the adult volunteers who run day-to-day operations (Bulletin 3 pp.78–84). IST are recruited through their contingents and assigned to jobs spanning Programme, Safety/Security/Medical, Site Operations, Food Services and Retail, Subcamps and Hubs, Accreditation and Guest Services, Contingent Relations, Transport, Ceremonies, and Media and Communications.

- IST theme dimensions: Bravely Explore, Bravely Act, Bravely Overcome, Bravely Lead.
- Program areas to choose from: Adventure, Chill Out, Culture, Fun, Innovation.
- Training in three phases: Base Camp (e-learning), Explorer Tracks (on-site role training), Horizon Exploration (reflection, networking, legacy). All IST complete IST Induction and Safe from Harm e-learning; a digital certificate recognizes participation.
- Key dates: IST Job Catalogue March 2026; general IST e-learning May 2026; job selection 15 May 2026 – 1 February 2027; job allocation notified April 2027; job-specific online training April–June 2027 and on-site training July 2027.

## Food

Food operates on two models (Bulletin 3 pp.85–89):

- Participants: a food-market model. Four food markets (one per hub) supply ingredients; each unit nominates representatives to collect ingredients for three daily meals and cooks in-unit. A collaborative WSJ 2027 Camp Cookbook offers balanced recipes.
- IST: a central dining hall run by a professional caterer; breakfast and dinner served, lunch as takeaway collected at breakfast.

Dietary needs (preferences, faith-based, medical/allergy) are collected during personal-data registration; severe allergies and complex needs must be communicated in detail and early. The Jamboree promotes meat-reduced, environmentally responsible choices (not fully meat-free). Contingents may run International Food Houses in a shared Food Court; a dedicated Food Houses circular was due in early 2026.

## Health, Safety, Safe from Harm, and Accessibility

Health and medical (Bulletin 3 pp.90–92): a First Aid Point in every subcamp (day and night), 24-hour Hub Clinics with doctors, and a Main Clinic with X-ray, ultrasound, and laboratory services, plus mobile rescue teams and ambulances. A 24/7 Emergency Operations Centre coordinates response. Contingents must brief their members and share health conditions, medications (e.g. insulin storage), allergies, and emergency contacts. Pre-existing conditions are excluded from covered care – hence mandatory insurance (see `jamboree2027-official-reference.md`).

Safe from Harm (Bulletin 3 pp.93–95): safeguards youth and adults, reduces discrimination, and – for the first time – includes access and inclusion. All adults must complete a compulsory three-module Safe from Harm e-learning on the WOSM Scout Learning Zone; the certificate is required to enter the Jamboree. A youth learning experience (delivered by Unit/Patrol Leaders) is available from the second half of 2026 (recommended, not compulsory). Listening Ear services (100+ trained IST) and Safe Zones operate across the site. Each contingent must nominate a Safe from Harm emergency contact within its CMT.

Accessibility and Inclusion (Bulletin 3 pp.96–99): the goal is the most inclusive Jamboree in history, built on Universal Design, Accessibility, Inclusion, Equity, and Reasonable Adjustments, covering mobility, sensory, neurodiversity, dietary, mental-health, and identity/faith/culture needs. Heads of Contingent must provide all accessibility needs during registration or support cannot be guaranteed; a carer/helper does not change the 10-person patrol limit. Each CMT nominates an Access and Inclusion contact.

Conduct: the Jamboree is alcohol-free; Polish law applies (legal adulthood 18); breaches of the Code of Conduct or local law can lead to eviction without refund. Bulletin 4 publishes formal Codes of Conduct for adults and for youth participants (linked from the Jamboree website).
